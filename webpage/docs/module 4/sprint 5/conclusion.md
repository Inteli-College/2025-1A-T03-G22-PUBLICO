# Conclusion
## Sprint 20: Project COnsolidation

This work presented the development of an integrated solution based on Large Language Models (LLMs) aimed at supporting the analysis of public procurement tenders within a consulting company operating in the public sector. The primary objective of the project was to investigate how artificial intelligence could be applied to optimize internal pre-sales processes, specifically by reducing the time and effort required to interpret extensive bid documents and by improving the identification of technical and financial requirements relevant to Go / No-Go decision-making.

Based on the results obtained throughout the project, it can be concluded that the proposed objectives were achieved. The developed solution demonstrated technical feasibility and strategic relevance by enabling automated extraction, structuring, and contextual analysis of tender documents through the combined use of LLMs, vector databases, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). Controlled tests, model benchmarking, and end-to-end validation confirmed that the system is capable of producing accurate, traceable, and consistent outputs, aligned with the needs of pre-sales teams operating under strict deadlines and regulatory constraints.

From a business perspective, the project highlights the significant potential impact of adopting artificial intelligence to optimize document-intensive processes. Baseline metrics collected through internal surveys with software architects and the financial team at ATOS revealed a high operational cost and substantial opportunity cost associated with manual bid analysis. The projected results indicate that the solution could lead to substantial reductions in analysis time and operational expenses, while simultaneously increasing agility, consistency, and decision quality.

Even as a Minimum Viable Product (MVP), the solution provides strong evidence of its value as a decision-support tool capable of enhancing competitiveness in the public procurement market.

### Qualitative and Strategic Gains

In addition to quantitative benefits, the project delivered important qualitative gains. The availability of structured and summarized information improves transparency and confidence in decision-making, reduces reliance on individual expertise, and enables better allocation of senior professionals to higher-value strategic activities. These aspects are particularly relevant in the context of pre-sales operations, where errors or delays in early-stage analysis can directly impact revenue generation and market positioning.

### Recommendations and Future Work

Based on the lessons learned and the results obtained, several recommendations can be made regarding the future evolution of the solution:

Expansion of the document analysis scope to include additional document types, such as contracts, annexes, technical specifications, and compliance reports;

Improvements in document chunking strategies, including segmentation by logical sections, to enhance retrieval accuracy and contextual relevance;

Implementation of a quantitative matching mechanism, expressed as a percentage of alignment between tender requirements and corporate certifications, to support more objective Go / No-Go decisions;

Deeper integration with corporate systems such as document repositories, bid management tools, and CRM platforms to increase adoption and operational impact.

Scalability, Maintenance, and Knowledge Transfer

Regarding scalability and maintenance, the cloud-native architecture adopted in this project provides a solid foundation for future growth. The use of managed services and serverless infrastructure facilitates scalability, cost control, and operational resilience.

To ensure long-term sustainability, it is recommended that the solution be accompanied by comprehensive technical documentation, including architectural diagrams, API specifications, prompt design guidelines, and operational procedures. This documentation is essential to support knowledge transfer and enable the company’s internal technical teams to maintain, evolve, and extend the solution independently.

A structured knowledge transfer plan is also recommended as a next step. This plan should include technical walkthrough sessions, documentation repositories, and pilot usage with selected pre-sales teams, ensuring that both technical and business stakeholders fully understand the system’s capabilities and limitations. Such an approach would reduce dependency on external development and accelerate internal adoption.
